Influence of Popularity

Learn why viral content is not always trustworthy and how popularity on social media can influence people's judgment about whether information is credible.

01

What is Influence of Popularity?

Content that goes viral is often assumed to be credible simply because many people have liked, shared, or reacted to it. This is commonly known as the bandwagon effect. Artificial Intelligence can further amplify this problem by generating engaging content designed to spread rapidly, regardless of whether the information is true or false.

Reference: https://www.ibm.com/think/insights/10-ai-dangers-and-risks-and-how-to-manage-them
02

Concerns About Influence of Popularity

Viral Doesn't Mean True

Information can spread quickly because it is emotional, surprising, or controversial—not because it is accurate.

Bandwagon Effect

People often trust or share content simply because many others have already engaged with it.

False Public Perception

Viral misinformation can influence public opinion long before reliable fact-checkers are able to verify the information.

How to Detect Non-Credible Content

Verify the Author

Check whether the author has identifiable credentials and experience related to the topic.

Look for Supporting Sources

Reliable information usually provides references or links to trustworthy primary sources.

Check the Publication Date

Viral posts sometimes recycle outdated news to make old events appear recent.

Cross-Check Information

Compare the claim with multiple reliable news organizations before believing or sharing it.

Reference: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.06696
04

Tools to Detect Non-Credible Content

NewsGuard

Browser extension that provides credibility ratings for news websites directly in search results and social media platforms.

Wayback Machine

Allows users to view previous versions of webpages and identify suspicious edits or changes.

IFCN Fact Checkers

A network of verified fact-checking organizations that investigate misleading online claims.

Trusted News Sources

Compare reports from established news organizations before accepting viral information as true.
