Cross Checking

Learn how to verify information by comparing multiple credible sources before believing or sharing online content.

01

What is Cross Checking?

Verifying a claim, image, or video against multiple independent, credible sources before accepting it as true — including checking original publication dates, primary sources, and whether reputable outlets have corroborated the same story.

Reference: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bBOJJlP68qo
02

Concerns about Cross Checking

Rapid Spread

False claims can spread much faster than they can be verified by professional fact-checkers.

Public Influence

Viral misinformation may influence public opinion before accurate information becomes available.

Loss of Accuracy

Without verification, misleading information may continue circulating and appear credible.

03

How to Detect When Cross Checking is Needed

Find the Original Source

A claim should be cross checked if it does not clearly identify where the information originally came from.

Compare Other Sources

Search whether other reliable news organizations report the same information before accepting it.

Check the Date

Old stories are often reshared to create false impressions about current events.

Practice Lateral Reading

Open additional trusted websites and compare their information before deciding if a claim is credible.

Reference: https://deakin.libguides.com/misinformation/dealing
04

Tools to Cross Check Credible Sources

Snopes

Investigates rumors, misinformation, and viral claims circulating online.

PolitiFact

Reviews statements from public officials and rates their factual accuracy.

FactCheck.org

Provides evidence supporting or debunking widely shared online claims.

Google Reverse Image Search / TinEye

Finds where an image first appeared and checks whether it is being reused out of context.
