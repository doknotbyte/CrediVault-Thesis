Social Validation

What is Social Validation and Verification Effort?

Social validation is the tendency to judge a claim's credibility based on social signals such as likes, shares, comment counts, or the idea that "everyone is talking about it" instead of checking the actual facts and evidence behind the claim.

Reference: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-02067-4
02

Concerns About Social Validation and Verification Effort

Unread Shares

Many links shared on social media are shared without the person actually clicking or reading the information first.

Engagement Can Mislead

A large number of likes, comments, or shares can make a post appear credible even when those numbers do not prove that the information is true.

False Sense of Knowledge

People may feel more knowledgeable about a topic after sharing an article even when they have not actually read the article.

How to Detect When Verification Effort is Missing

Do Not Rely on Engagement

High numbers of likes, shares, or comments do not automatically mean that a claim is accurate.

Look for the Original Source

Be cautious when a post provides no link to the original article, document, statement, or evidence.

Read Before Sharing

Click the link and read the full information instead of relying only on the headline or caption.

Check Independent Sources

Search for information from other reliable and independent sources before accepting the claim.

Reference: https://texasstandard.org/stories/university-texas-researchers-study-sharing-stories-social-media-without-reading/
04

Tools and Practices to Improve Verification

Lateral Reading

Open other sources and compare what independent websites say instead of relying only on engagement numbers.

Read Before You Share

Pause before sharing and read the complete article, not only the headline, caption, or preview.

Snopes / FactCheck.org

Check whether a highly shared claim has already been reviewed by established fact-checking sources.

Check Post History

Examine an account's previous posts and track record before treating a highly engaged post as trustworthy.
