Source & Evidence Verification

Learn how to verify the origin of information and determine whether claims are supported by credible evidence before accepting or sharing them online.

Learning Progress Topic 1 of 2
01

What is Source and Evidence Verification?

Source and evidence verification means confirming that a claim, image, or statement comes from an identifiable and accountable origin and that it is supported by demonstrable evidence, such as documents, data, or firsthand accounts, rather than assumption or repetition.

Reference: https://researchguides.uoregon.edu/medialiteracy/evaluating-sources
02

Concerns About Source and Evidence Verification

Repeated Information

Without checking sources, content can circulate mainly because it has been repeatedly shared rather than because its original source has been confirmed.

Unclear Origins

Screenshots, reposts, and paraphrases can hide the original source, making it difficult to determine who created the information and whether the source is accountable.

Loss of Context

Each retelling can remove important details, change the meaning of the original information, or leave out the context needed to understand the claim correctly.

03

How to Detect When Source Verification is Needed

Check the Original Source

Verify information when the supposed source is only a screenshot, repost, or paraphrase instead of a link to the original document, statement, or footage.

Examine the Evidence

Check whether the facts and claims are supported by actual evidence and determine whether the available information holds up when examined carefully.

Identify the Source

Determine who created or published the information, whether the source is identifiable, and whether the source can be considered trustworthy.

Check the Context

Look beyond the claim and determine whether the information represents the complete story or whether important details have been removed.

Consider the Audience

Identify who the information is intended to reach and consider how the target audience may affect the way the message is presented.

Identify the Purpose

Ask why the information was created and determine whether its purpose is to inform, persuade, influence, entertain, or achieve another objective.

Reference: https://researchguides.uoregon.edu/medialiteracy/evaluating-sources
04

Tools to Verify Sources and Evidence

Google Reverse Image Search

Use reverse image search to trace an image or photo and identify earlier or original appearances of the same visual content.

TinEye

Search for earlier appearances of an image across the internet and compare where and when the image has previously been published.

PolitiFact / FactCheck.org

Check statements made by public figures and compare their claims with available evidence and verified information.

Snopes

Investigate the origin and credibility of specific viral claims and misleading information circulating online.

Government & Court Websites

Go directly to government, court, or institutional websites to access primary documents instead of relying only on summaries or reposts.

Institutional Sources

Check credible institutional websites and original publications when verifying claims, documents, reports, or official information.
