What is Clickbait?

Headlines or thumbnails designed to provoke curiosity or emotion rather than accurately represent the content, often exaggerated to drive clicks regardless of the story's actual substance. Artificial Intelligence can now automatically generate large volumes of clickbait content, making it increasingly difficult to recognize online.

Concerns about Clickbait

Cybersecurity Risks

Some clickbait pages redirect users to phishing websites, malware downloads, or fraudulent online services.

Misleading Information

Readers often waste time opening articles whose content does not match the exaggerated headline.

Massive Online Spread

Clickbait has become common even among popular social media pages and some mainstream news platforms.

How to Detect Clickbait

Sensational Headlines

Clickbait often uses exaggerated words, dramatic phrases, or shocking statements designed to trigger curiosity or emotions.

Curiosity Gap

Headlines that say "You Won't Believe..." or intentionally hide important information are common clickbait techniques.

Read Beyond the Headline

Always read the article itself and verify whether the content actually supports the headline being presented.

Check the Source

Evaluate whether the website is trustworthy and whether other reliable sources report the same information.

Reference: https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/13/4/2456
04

Tools to Detect Clickbait

Clickbait Detector

Chrome extension and GitHub project that analyzes headlines and estimates how clickbait they appear.

Nyckel Clickbait Identifier

AI-powered classifier that labels whether a headline is clickbait together with a confidence score.

Hugging Face Models

Machine-learning models trained to identify sensationalized headlines using Natural Language Processing.

Manual Verification

Compare the headline with multiple trusted news sources before believing or sharing the information.
